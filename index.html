The uncomfortable part

Nobody’s behavior ever changed because they sat in a classroom.

Knowledge transfer is cheap, measurable, and almost useless on its own. What moves a business metric is practice in the real environment. Under the real pressure. On the real system. In front of the real customer.
That costs money. Which is exactly why the Kingdom funds it, and why only ten percent of what we design is classroom at all.

Why you were right to be skeptical
Bought from a catalog

An expense.

  • Starts from a catalog
  • One day in a room
  • Mixed starting knowledge
  • Attendance recorded as the outcome
  • No baseline, so no change can ever be proven
  • Nothing happens after the room empties
Built as a system

An investment.

  • Starts from your strategy and your numbers
  • Separates a skill gap from a process gap
  • Prepares before, practices in the room, applies after
  • Re-measures against the baseline it started from
The difference is not the quality of the content. It is where the work starts, and whether anyone closes the loop.

So we build it the other way around

If the classroom does not change behavior, stop spending there.

The Arabian Academy design ratio
70%
Practice and application
Field experience, real work, skill and readiness

Ten percent of the design is theory. That is not a shortcut. It is the reason the other ninety percent has somewhere to land.
So where does the investment actually go?

An academy, not a training room.

Frontline

Build the environment, not the slide deck.

  • Store mockups with real fixtures and real merchandising
  • Live POS terminals on the training floor
  • Stockroom and replenishment simulation
  • Computer labs for systems and product mastery
  • Trainers who have stood on that floor themselves
Leadership

Build the follow-through, not the workshop.

  • On-floor support during live operating hours
  • Structured coaching against observed behavior
  • Mentoring cycles tied to a named business metric
  • People managers trained as the assessors
  • Field coaches with protected, budgeted capacity
You cannot buy the seventy percent out of a catalog. You have to build it.

Level 5. Two formulas.

BCR = Program Benefits ÷ Program Costs ROI (%) = Net Program Benefits ÷ Program Costs × 100 Net Program Benefits = Program Benefits − Program Costs
Principle 04
“Select the most conservative alternative for calculations.”
Principle 05
“Use at least one method to isolate the effects of a project.”
Principle 10
“Fully load all costs of a solution, project, or program when analyzing ROI.”

ابنِ البيئة، لا العرض التقديمي.

  • نماذج متاجر بتجهيزات حقيقية وعرض بضاعة حقيقي
  • أجهزة نقاط بيع فعلية داخل صالة التدريب
  • محاكاة للمستودع وإعادة التزويد
  • معامل حاسب لإتقان الأنظمة والمنتجات
  • مدرّبون وقفوا على تلك الصالة بأنفسهم
القيادة

ابنِ المتابعة، لا ورشة العمل.

  • مساندة ميدانية أثناء ساعات التشغيل الفعلية
  • إرشاد منظّم مقابل سلوك ملحوظ
  • دورات إرشاد مرتبطة بمؤشر أعمال محدّد
  • تأهيل المديرين المباشرين ليكونوا هم المقيّمين
  • مدرّبون ميدانيون بطاقة محمية ومدرجة في الميزانية
